Research

Safe, interpretable, and adaptive AI for systems that act in the physical world

I develop safe, interpretable, and adaptive AI systems for real-world cyber-physical environments that must operate under uncertainty, strict physical constraints, and adversarial conditions. My work sits at the intersection of safe reinforcement learning, physics-informed AI, probabilistic modeling, and AI safety evaluation, with a core focus on building systems that are robust, verifiable, and deployable at scale in safety-critical domains.

Research Vision

Long-term goal Build AI systems that are provably beneficial, auditable, and aligned with human values at scale: agents that stay robust under distribution shift and adversarial conditions, can be interpreted and corrected, and operate reliably in safety-critical environments.

The most important open problems in AI are not only about capability. They are about safety, verification, and alignment. My path into these problems runs through safety-critical control: in power grids, a learning-based controller that fails under noise or attack has physical consequences. That experience shapes how I approach AI safety more broadly. I treat constraints, uncertainty, and worst-case behavior as first-class design requirements, and I test systems for how they fail, not only for how well they perform.

My research agenda builds on three directions:

01

Safe & constrained reinforcement learning

Question
How do we give RL agents safety guarantees that still hold under distribution shift and adversarial perturbation?
Approach
Formal safety constraints, physics-informed inductive biases, certified robustness, and safe exploration, validated on large-scale cyber-physical systems.
02

Scalable AI oversight

Question
How can we systematically catch reward hacking, sycophancy, and goal misgeneralization in deployed agentic systems?
Approach
Evaluation frameworks and behavioral benchmarks for agents, and safety monitors that transfer across models and settings.
03

Mechanistic interpretability & deceptive alignment

Question
Can we locate and detect hidden objectives, such as evaluation awareness, inside a model's internals?
Approach
Sparse autoencoders, activation patching, steering vectors (CAA), and probe transfer on open-weight language models.

Research Thrusts

Safe & Intelligent Systems for Smart Energy

How can learning-based controllers keep a power grid stable when they face noise, faults, and attacks?

Physics-informed deep RL, safe and uncertainty-aware control, LLM-guided planning, and sim-to-real transfer for smart grids and DER-integrated energy systems. Agents remain reliable under sensor noise, hardware faults, non-stationarity, and adversarial perturbations.

Volt-VAR ControlPhysics-Informed RLUncertainty QuantificationLLM ControlDomain Adaptation
Key work: physics-based DRL for grid-resilient Volt-VAR control (IEEE TSG, under review) · attack-resilient DRL Volt-VAR control (ISGT 2024) · Bayesian BiLSTM wind forecasting with UQ (EPSR 2026)

AI Safety & Alignment

Can we tell when an AI agent behaves differently because it knows it is being evaluated?

Behavioral and mechanistic evaluations to detect deceptive alignment, reward hacking, and eval-awareness in LLM agents, and scalable oversight frameworks for agentic systems in safety-critical deployments.

Evaluation AwarenessRed-TeamingScalable OversightMonitor Transferability
Current work: stress-testing eval-aware safety monitors, supported by an NSF ACCESS ACCELERATE allocation (CIS260799)

Vision & Simulation for Autonomous Systems

How do we carry policies trained in simulation into the real world without losing safety?

End-to-end perception-control pipelines, multimodal sensor fusion, and sim-to-real transfer using CARLA, AirSim, and OpenDSS for autonomous and cyber-physical systems.
